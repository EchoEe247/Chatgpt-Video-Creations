# 2026-09-28 user quality thresholds and current cinematic candidates

## Purpose

Clarify the repository's maturity philosophy and record the user's exact current quality judgments using the latest/current named productions rather than generic labels or stale earlier attempts.

## Quality-language contract

**Respectful** is an intermediate production-quality threshold. It means the exact candidate is worth keeping, worth continuing to improve, and potentially worth releasing later. It does **not** mean finished, release-ready, accepted canon, or baseline-promoted.

Technical validity, reproducibility, hashes, tests, deterministic rendering, and automation can establish process control. They do not by themselves establish that a creative production method is mature enough for `baseline → operate`.

## Exact current examples

- **RIDGELINE — A Descent** — latest mountain-bike candidate: **respectful**. Keep and continue; not declared finished.
- **VELOCITY — A Highway Study** — latest reviewed highway-driving recreation in `productions/standalone/velocity-recreation`: **respectful**. Keep and continue; not declared finished.
- **LAX — Final Approach** — current airplane candidate: **below respectful / currently closer to bad, but potentially salvageable**. The airplane itself is not the main failure. The environment needs substantially more completeness/realism; direction/kinematics and flight-to-touchdown/ground-roll mechanics need to read correctly, including better landing-gear/wheel behavior; aviation audio must match the physical phases; and visual quality needs to remain impressive throughout.
- **AETHERFALL — THE LAST LIGHT** (`aetherfall-last-light.mp4`) — **medium-bad**. Its environment was stronger than the current LAX environment, but the Avatar-replication concept did not land, the characters needed improvement, and audio needed improvement.
- **THE EIGHTH HOUR** — Severance-inspired candidate: **trash / failed**. Failure evidence only.

There is still **no accepted realistic/cinematic visual baseline**.

## Review-player requirement

Any user-facing review page must expose a working, unlocked seek bar / scrubber / timeline. The reviewer must be able to drag freely to any timestamp, rewind, jump forward, pause, and inspect arbitrary sections without replaying the film linearly.
