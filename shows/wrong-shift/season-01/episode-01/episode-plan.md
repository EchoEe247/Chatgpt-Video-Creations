# Wrong Shift S01E01 — Floor -0

## Status
IN_PRODUCTION

## Target
Approximately 8 minutes. 1280×720. 24 fps delivery. Scene-based limited 2D animation. Distinct recurring voices. Dialogue-first 48 kHz stereo mix.

## Episode function
This is the first candidate canon episode. It must establish the show engine without over-explaining the season mystery.

## Story
A residential building's elevator begins showing an impossible `-0` floor between the lobby and basement. Ms. Hale, a tenant, reports that the elevator sometimes opens onto a version of the lobby with no people, no outside doors, and clocks stuck at 3:17.

Rae treats `-0` as a continuity residue: a floor index created when two valid building states briefly occupied the same registration slot. Owen is fascinated by the fact that the elevator appears to remember a floor the building never had.

They enter the residue and discover an abandoned RealityCare service alcove containing old field tape and a maintenance warning: `DO NOT CLOSE BOTH DOORS.` A local service record lists Rae and Owen as the technicians, dated six months in the future.

The anomaly escalates when the normal elevator door and the residue door begin synchronizing. If both boundaries close at once, the system will choose one registered building state and discard the other. Rae and Owen stabilize the fault by physically holding one boundary open while Rae reindexes the elevator with orange field tape. The repair intentionally leaves a tiny unresolved continuity gap rather than forcing a “perfect” closure.

Back in the normal lobby, the elevator behaves correctly. Owen's tablet marks the service call as `VISIT 2 OF 2`. Rae tells him not to sync the record. The final tag returns to the night office, where Rae discovers an unfamiliar strip of orange field tape in her pocket marked: `NEXT TIME, LET OWEN CLOSE IT.` She hides it instead of telling him.

## Character beats
Rae: competent and restrained; her first real fear appears when the future service record names them. She still chooses containment over disclosure.

Owen: curious but useful; his willingness to stay physically engaged with the anomaly is what makes the repair possible. He is not the fool of the episode.

Ms. Hale: exhausted customer who frames the impossible elevator as a building-management problem.
