# Wrong Shift — World

## RealityCare
RealityCare is a private infrastructure company responsible for continuity faults: localized failures of time, space, identity, causality, duplication, and related phenomena. Public-facing language is deliberately mundane: faults become tickets, anomalies become customer impact, and existential danger becomes service severity.

RealityCare is not omnipotent. It has procedures, equipment, liability rules, budget constraints, rival internal teams, bad documentation, and unknown legacy systems.

## Night shift
Rae and Owen work the lower-priority overnight maintenance lane. They receive small or ambiguous incidents that often become more serious once inspected. Their equipment is functional but older than day-shift gear.

## Field tape
Orange field tape marks active repair boundaries and temporary safe states. Red tape marks legal/containment conditions where ordinary technicians should stop and escalate. Tape can appear as a recurring visual and narrative clue. A future or overwritten crew may leave tape that survives timeline changes.

## Continuity faults
Useful recurring categories:
- spatial: impossible rooms, duplicate spaces, doors opening somewhere else;
- temporal: future/past leakage, repeated minutes, impossible service dates;
- identity: duplicates, overwritten memories, people who exist in only one version;
- causal: effects preceding causes, repairs that create the fault they solve;
- archival: RealityCare systems retaining records from overwritten timelines.

An anomaly must have understandable local rules. Strange does not mean arbitrary.

## Repair principle
Repairs stabilize a local continuity relationship rather than magically erase all consequences. The crew should usually discover a rule, test it, and perform a physical/procedural repair that viewers can follow.

## Episode 1 location contract
S01E01 introduces a residential lobby/elevator and the impossible floor “-0.” Floor -0 is not another universe in full; it is a continuity residue between registered floors, containing traces of overwritten maintenance states.

## Recurring environmental humor
RealityCare and affected locations may contain small readable details such as:
- forms for impossible incidents;
- safety signage that treats paradoxes as workplace hazards;
- vending/coffee interfaces with temporally wrong options;
- clocks, floor labels, receipts, or ticket states that reveal subtle continuity errors.

These details support the world but must not break text-safe areas or distract from dialogue.

## Hard continuity rules
- Accepted physical set geometry remains consistent unless the story explicitly changes it.
- Character feet use set floor/contact anchors rather than sprite bounding boxes.
- Props and anomaly effects inherit parent/set anchors rather than being positioned ad hoc per shot.
- Camera transforms project shared world geometry; close-ups do not invent replacement coordinates.
- A closed ticket or future artifact is evidence, not automatically proof of one final explanation.
