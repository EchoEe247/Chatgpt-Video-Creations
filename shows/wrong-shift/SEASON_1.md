# Wrong Shift — Season 1 Plan

## Season theme
Maintenance versus responsibility. Rae and Owen begin by assuming that every continuity problem is a local fault with a local repair. Over the season they discover that repairs themselves can survive across overwritten timelines and become causes of later faults.

## Beginning state
- Rae trusts containment procedure more than explanation.
- Owen still believes good technicians can make RealityCare a good institution.
- They treat strange tickets as isolated jobs.
- Neither believes they have worked together before Owen's current employment.

## End state
- Both know RealityCare records can survive overwritten timelines.
- They have direct evidence that versions of themselves performed repairs they do not remember.
- Rae can no longer dismiss Owen's questions as rookie curiosity.
- Owen no longer assumes that fixing the immediate anomaly necessarily fixes the underlying problem.
- The season ends with a maintenance event that proves their own actions can become future causes.

## Episode map

### S01E01 — Floor -0
A residential elevator begins opening onto an impossible floor between registered levels. Rae and Owen discover a continuity residue containing RealityCare equipment and a warning tied to their own future service record. They stabilize the elevator by deliberately leaving one continuity boundary unresolved. End breadcrumb: Owen's tablet marks the visit as `2 OF 2` even though this is their first visit.

Purpose: establish the repair engine, Rae/Owen contrast, RealityCare rules, recurring field-tape language, and the first undeniable evidence of recursive maintenance.

### S01E02 — Lost Before Found
A RealityCare vending/return machine begins dispensing possessions before their owners lose them. A future object belonging to Rae appears with service markings she cannot explain.

Dependency: builds on E01's impossible future-service evidence without repeating the same elevator mechanism.

### S01E03 — Quiet Hours
An apartment bedroom removes consequences from anything done inside it until the door opens. Owen's attempt to help a customer creates a small permanent continuity change that survives the repair.

Purpose: prove consequences persist beyond clues and deepen Owen's empathy-driven risk.

### S01E04 — Two-Day Shipping
A package arrives from a delivery route that no longer exists. The contents reference a neighborhood erased from current maps but remembered by RealityCare's archive.

Purpose: widen the mystery from personal future evidence to overwritten public history.

### S01E05 — Customer Impact
A fault forces Rae to open the customer-impact record she normally avoids. The episode reveals that an earlier repair in her career erased someone from ordinary memory while RealityCare retained the case.

Purpose: explain Rae's containment-first behavior without resolving every detail of her past.

### S01E06 — Day Shift
Rae and Owen are paired with a polished day-shift crew. The day team has better equipment and incomplete records; the night team has worse equipment but access to anomaly evidence the day team never sees.

Purpose: complicate the idea that management or day shift simply knows everything.

### S01E07 — Closed Before Open
A ticket is already closed when it arrives. Following the service trail leads Rae and Owen through repairs apparently performed by future versions of themselves.

Purpose: convert season breadcrumbs into a direct recursive-causality problem.

### S01E08 — Maintenance Window
RealityCare schedules a city-scale continuity patch. Rae and Owen discover that performing the official sequence exactly would erase evidence and recreate one of the faults they have been investigating. They choose an imperfect repair that preserves the current timeline but creates a new unresolved state.

Final image/thread: a fresh ticket appears bearing their own handwriting before either of them writes it.

## Setup/payoff dependencies
- E01 `VISIT 2 OF 2` -> E07/E08 proof that service chronology is recursive.
- E01 warning about not closing both boundaries -> recurring rule that “complete” repairs can be more dangerous than intentionally incomplete ones.
- Rae's tape-checking tell -> E05 emotional reveal.
- Owen's willingness to test procedure -> initially causes risk, later becomes necessary in E08.
- RealityCare archival persistence -> introduced subtly in E01, widened in E04, central in E07/E08.

## Season guardrails
- Do not explain the full origin of RealityCare in Season 1.
- Do not prove that every anomaly is deliberately created by the company.
- Do not make future selves omniscient.
- Do not erase accepted episode consequences without an explicit story cost.
- Every mystery clue must remain compatible with at least two plausible explanations until late season.
