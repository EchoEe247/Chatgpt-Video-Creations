# Wrong Shift — Characters

## Rae
Role: veteran RealityCare night-shift technician.

Surface read: composed, dry, competent, procedural.

Deeper rule: Rae is not merely cynical. She has learned that curiosity can create consequences, so she often chooses containment over explanation. She quietly protects Owen while denying that she is doing so.

Behavior:
- closed, economical posture;
- small deliberate gestures;
- rarely wastes movement;
- straightens or checks field tape when anxious;
- looks at exits, equipment, or containment boundaries before reacting emotionally.

Strengths: pattern recognition, procedural knowledge, calm under pressure.

Flaws: over-trusts containment habits, withholds context, can mistake resignation for wisdom.

Season thread: Rae has prior history with a continuity incident she refuses to discuss. RealityCare records and future artifacts increasingly suggest that she has participated in repairs she does not remember.

Voice identity: clearly distinct female voice, lighter than Owen, controlled and dry. Brisk but measured; clarity first. Do not cast her with the same effective timbre as Owen.

## Owen
Role: newer night-shift technician.

Surface read: curious, optimistic, expressive, still reacts like an ordinary person to impossible events.

Deeper rule: Owen is not incompetent. His optimism makes him willing to test assumptions that Rae treats as settled. Sometimes this causes trouble; sometimes it exposes that RealityCare procedure is wrong.

Behavior:
- wider posture and larger gestures;
- leans toward anomalies instead of away from them;
- uses his company tablet constantly;
- visible recoil and surprise;
- asks the question Rae would rather leave unanswered.

Strengths: lateral thinking, empathy, willingness to challenge procedure.

Flaws: curiosity, emotional commitment to “fixing” things that may need containment, desire to prove he belongs.

Season thread: Owen originally applied for day shift and was rejected as “too emotionally reactive.” He still wants to believe RealityCare can be made better rather than merely endured.

Voice identity: distinct deeper male voice. Faster and more energetic than Rae, but stable and intelligible. The deeper tone belongs to Owen in the current casting direction.

## Ms. Hale
Role: one-off S01E01 residential customer.

Read: tired resident who treats the impossible elevator as a property-management problem because she has already spent hours dealing with it.

Voice identity: third, clearly distinct older/lower-energy female voice. Must not sound like Rae.

## Relationship engine
Rae teaches Owen how continuity repair actually works. Owen forces Rae to examine why she accepts RealityCare rules. Neither should become the permanent fool or permanent correct one.

Dialogue should preserve this asymmetry:
- Rae compresses information.
- Owen expands questions.
- Rae reacts to risk first.
- Owen reacts to meaning first.
