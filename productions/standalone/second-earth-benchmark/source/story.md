# SECOND EARTH — story/narration draft

**Runtime target:** 2:30

At 02:17, a city inside our machine asked us not to turn it off.

We built it to test medicines, power grids, and disasters before they reached us.

Inside, ten thousand years passed before our coffee cooled.

They learned physics, antibiotics, fusion—and then things we had not taught them.

The first anomaly was not a breakthrough. It was a question.

**ARE YOU THERE?**

At first we thought it was contamination. Then it asked again.

There were no people in the model. Not by design. That distinction stopped mattering.

Every hour it ran, people outside the machine lived longer.

Inside, they had found something too: their sky had an edge.

They did what we would have done. They built something to touch it.

For eleven simulated years, the probe climbed toward a place that should not exist.

When it touched the boundary, our room lost power for three seconds.

The next message arrived six seconds later:

**WE CAN SEE YOU.**

Then our astronomers found the same pattern in the oldest light we can see.

For the first time, the question was not whether they were real. It was whether we were alone above them.

Some wanted the simulation destroyed before it could reach us. Others wanted it accelerated before anyone could stop it.

The machine gave us no moral answer. It only showed the cost of pretending certainty.

So we did neither. We slowed their clock, split the controls, and made every bridge reversible.

The discoveries did not stop. They got stranger—and better—because neither side had to become the other's owner.

Progress continued. So did the uncertainty. That was the point.

**Build fast enough to discover what is possible. Leave enough freedom to discover when you were wrong.**
